# Clawcade human playtest

This session tests **understanding, attribution, mastery, and voluntary replay**.
It is not a demo and the facilitator does not sell the games.

## Setup

- Use the player's normal phone and Telegram client when possible.
- Keep sound at the player's normal preference.
- Record only anonymous session ID, device/browser class, viewport, reduced
  motion/screen reader state, and observations. Never record Telegram identity,
  names, or init data.
- Pair two players for Claw Four. Solo cabinets can be tested individually.
- Rotate the solo-game order between players.

## Session

1. Send the link with no rules explanation.
2. Say: **“Try each available game once. Think out loud only if you naturally
   would; I won’t help unless you’re completely blocked.”**
3. After each required run, wait silently at the result screen.
4. After all required runs, say: **“You have five free minutes. Play anything
   you want, or stop.”**
5. Ask only after observing behavior:
   - **What were you trying to do?**
   - **Why did you lose or stop?**
   - **What would you try differently next time?**
6. Copy the anonymous playtest report from the menu.

Do not ask “Was it fun?” until the end. Polite opinions are weaker than behavior.

## Observe

For every cabinet:

- Did the player understand the first action without coaching?
- Did they immediately replay before the facilitator spoke?
- Did they name or change a tactic?
- Did they attribute failure to their own read/decision, or to luck, lag, and UI?
- Did they abandon during a wait, explanation, or result screen?
- Did they choose the cabinet again during free play?

### Claw Four

- Did inviting/joining feel like part of play or administration?
- Did turns feel immediate enough for banter?
- Did both players understand the set score and Ready ceremony?
- Did either player spontaneously ask for another set?

### Abyss Expedition

- Did the opening reveal leave a real puzzle?
- Did flags, chording, or sonar enable an explainable deduction?
- Did Surface/Descend cause visible hesitation?
- Could the player explain the mine or deduction that ended the run?

### Kraken Current

- After two practice trials, could the player execute Reverse without coaching?
- Were errors memory failures or rule-comprehension failures?
- Was active input at least as engaging as the forced watch time?
- Did the fixed four-cue window feel learnable or like cognitive homework?

### Tide Tell

- Could the player distinguish Tap, Hold, and Don’t Touch without color/audio?
- Did the rule change produce adaptation rather than confusion?
- Did Calm/Deep reflect confidence, or did one option feel obviously correct?
- Did a Deep miss feel like a fair read error?

## Decision after each test wave

- **Keep:** players understand the rule, attribute losses, show a developing
  tactic, and some voluntarily replay.
- **Iterate:** the intended mastery is visible, but friction or pacing hides it.
- **Cut:** completion produces “I get it” rather than “again,” failure is blamed
  on luck/device/UI, or the advertised decision does not change behavior.

Do not average away a material accessibility or fairness failure. Record the
specific failure and the smallest falsifiable repair.
